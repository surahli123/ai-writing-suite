# Search Ranking Test Readout: Pinewood Books

## Summary

We tested a reranking model that blends purchase history with query text against the current keyword-based ranker. Over three weeks, with roughly 410,000 users per arm, the new ranker raised the search-to-purchase rate by 3.1% relative (from 5.80% to 5.98%) with no meaningful change in page load time. We recommend shipping it to all web users in the week of 7 December.

## Background

Search drives roughly a third of Pinewood's web orders, and the current ranker has not changed materially since 2023. Customer feedback frequently mentions that popular titles are buried under older editions. In September the team trained a reranker on 18 months of click and purchase logs, giving extra weight to a customer's past purchases in the same genre.

## Method

Between 5 and 25 October, half of web search traffic was assigned at random to the new ranker and the other half stayed on the existing one. Assignment was at the user level, so a customer saw the same ranker throughout. Each arm included about 401,000 users. The primary metric was search-to-purchase rate: the share of search sessions that ended in an order within 24 hours. Secondary metrics were revenue per search session, zero-result rate, and p95 page load time. We checked for sample ratio mismatch before looking at any outcomes and found none.

## Results

Search-to-purchase rate was 5.80% in control and 5.98% in treatment, an absolute difference of 0.18 percentage points and a relative lift of 3.1%. The 95% confidence interval for the absolute difference is 0.09 to 0.27 points. Revenue per search session rose by 1.9%, though the interval for that metric includes zero. The zero-result rate was unchanged at 2.4%. The p95 page load time increased from 1.42 to 1.44 seconds, which we do not consider material.

## Caveats

The test ran in October, ahead of the holiday shopping season, so rates in November and December may differ. Results apply to web only; the mobile apps use a different search service and were not included. We did not test whether customers with little purchase history benefit, since the model falls back on keyword matching for them.

## Next steps

Engineering will complete the load test by 20 November. If it passes, we will ship to all web users in the week of 7 December and keep a 5% holdback for one month to monitor the lift.

# Store Labor Scheduling Pilot: Findings

## Summary

Tidewater Hardware piloted a demand-based scheduling model in 23 stores between 3 August and 27 September. Understaffed hours fell from 14.2% to 9.8% of open hours in pilot stores, while labor spend stayed within 1% of plan. Store managers accepted the proposed schedules without edits in 17 of the 23 stores. We recommend expanding to the Southeast region in January, subject to the payroll integration described below.

## What we did

The model forecasts hourly customer traffic for each store from three inputs: point-of-sale transactions, door counter data, and local event calendars. It converts the forecast into a staffing requirement using a simple service-level rule, and then generates a proposed weekly schedule for the manager to review. Managers could accept the schedule as is or edit it. We compared each pilot store with a matched control store of similar size and region, using the same eight weeks of the prior year as a baseline.

## Results

Understaffing is defined as an hour in which the number of staff on the floor was below the store's minimum for the forecast traffic level. Across the 23 pilot stores it fell from 14.2% to 9.8% of open hours. In the matched control stores it moved from 14.0% to 13.1%, so the net improvement attributable to the model is about 3.5 percentage points.

Labor spend in pilot stores was 0.6% above plan, compared with 0.9% above plan in control stores. We do not read much into the difference.

Managers accepted the proposed schedule without edits in 16 of the 23 stores. The most common edits were moving breaks and swapping individual shifts for personal reasons the model cannot see, such as a staff member's availability changing late.

## Limitations

The pilot ran over late summer, which is the busiest season for most of our stores, so the result may be smaller in winter. Two stores in the pilot had a manager change in the middle of the period. Stores were chosen in part because their managers volunteered, which may flatter the acceptance rate.

## Next steps

Payroll integration is the main blocker. Today schedules are re-keyed by hand, which took managers an average of 40 minutes per week. Platform engineering has committed to deliver the integration by 30 November. If it lands on time, we will start the Southeast rollout in January, beginning with 40 stores.
